Nothing is asked for until it pays for itself An account buys saving and syncing, so it is asked for when they first save or go apart. A partner link buys distance, so it is asked for when they are apart. Limits buy safety, so they are captured the first time someone taps "too far" — not in a twenty-card wizard at midnight.
Heat is a thumb, not a form Three levels, always on screen, changeable mid-card. The engine's five intensity levels stay underneath; the person never meets them.
The card is the product One instruction, big, in a face with some warmth to it, with the heat showing in the colour. No shape screens, no chapter counts, no "what's inside stays sealed" — just the thing to do next.
Every no is silent and instant Skip needs no reason. "Too far" needs no explanation and never reaches the partner. That is the existing privacy architecture, finally visible as a feature instead of a setup step.
What survives from the current build The server engine, the boundary filter, the rules matrix, the data-only notifications, the burn-after-reading media, the couple-scoped entitlement. All of it stays. It moves behind the play instead of standing in front of it.
Tap through the phone — every button works. Content shown is sample copy at each heat level, written to the same authoring rules as the real packs. The five games on Home map to the existing content/games.json primitives, so adding one stays a content edit rather than a screen.
