Afterhours · the visual rebuild

A dim room. Not a dark app.

A dark app is a light app with the colours inverted: flat panels, hairline borders, one gap between everything. A dim room has a source of light, things that catch it, texture, and shadow where the light does not reach. Today's build is the first thing. This is the second — the foundations first, then every screen drawn on top of them.

§4.9 — Icons, because emoji are the loudest amateur signal left

🔥 🎲 🌙 💌 👀 📖 ⏱ come out. One set: 1.5 dp stroke, 24 dp box, round caps, no fills. Uniformly plain beats individually pretty. A Konsist rule fails the build on an emoji literal in a user-facing string.

D4 · The card and the player

The worst screen in the app is the product itself.

In today's golden the glow bands into a hard pink rectangle, the title sits directly on the body, three calls to action compete, and the bottom 40% of the screen is empty. Everything a thumb needs is in the middle.

D5 · Home

The same home for everyone, finally finished.

The structure does not change — that is the U1 claim, and it holds. What changes is that the primary action docks within thumb reach, sections get real rhythm, rows stop pretending to be cards, and the live card becomes the one bloomed object on the screen.

D6 · Games

Every primitive on the same card. No game gets its own language.

A draw lands on the card. A dial is a track across its width. A recording is the mic in the dock. The hand-off is the only screen in the app with no chrome at all.

D7 · The rest

Every screen through the same frame.

Plan, apart, the library, the account, pairing. Nothing here changes what any of them do.

D8 · Brand

The app currently has none.

A template launcher icon, no splash, no wordmark. The minimum a store listing requires and a home screen deserves — and no more.

§10

Done when all eight are true.

  1. Grey-box test. Replace every piece of text with a grey box and the hierarchy still reads.
  2. No two different kinds of thing share the same shape, fill and border.
  3. One primary action per screen, within thumb reach.
  4. No screen has more than 120 dp of unintended empty space.
  5. A Playful card and a Wild card are different objects.
  6. Not one emoji left in the app.
  7. Nothing is a Material 3 default.
  8. Two seconds on home is enough to say what the app is for.
Every phone on this page carries its own scene, rim light, grain and heat accent — the foundations are demonstrated, not drawn. The heat switch at the top is the §4.4 argument: one setting, and the whole room changes.

Nothing here changes behaviour. The flow is the one confirmed in U1–U6, and none of the privacy architecture moves: the server still filters, refusals are still silent, a dial's number still never leaves the phone, and a memory still holds no content id.
